# Gift Tax Exclusion 2026: How Couples Can Give $38,000 Per Person, Tax-Free

> The IRS lets you give $19,000 per recipient in 2026 with no gift tax and no paperwork. Married couples can double that to $38,000. Here's how the annual exclusion works — and the December 31 deadline that actually matters.

Reviewed: October 11, 2026

## The number to know: $19,000

For 2026, the IRS annual gift tax exclusion is **$19,000 per recipient** — the same as in 2025. That means one person can give $19,000 to any number of people during the calendar year, and none of those gifts triggers a gift tax return or a dollar of gift tax.

A married couple can effectively double it: each spouse gets their own $19,000 exclusion, so together they can give **$38,000 to a single recipient** with no filing and no tax. Three kids and four grandkids? That's seven people times $38,000 — up to $266,000 moved in one year, entirely within the exclusion.

## How gift-splitting works for couples

Two spouses have two clean routes to that $38,000 per person:

- **Each spouse gives $19,000 from their own funds.** Two separate transfers, two separate exclusions, no paperwork at all.
- **Gift splitting.** One spouse gives the full $38,000 and both spouses formally agree to split it — but that election requires filing Form 709 for the year.

If you want zero paperwork, the two-separate-gifts route is simpler.

## The deadline that actually matters

The annual exclusion is use-it-or-lose-it. A 2026 gift has to be **complete by December 31, 2026**. The exclusion resets every January 1, and anything you didn't use is gone forever — it never carries forward.

This is the real deadline for year-end planning. If you intended to help an adult child with a down payment or fund a grandchild's account this year, a gift completed in December counts for 2026; one completed in January counts for 2027. The IRS cares about when the transfer is complete, not when you decided to make it.

## What happens if you give more than $19,000

Giving more than the exclusion to one person doesn't mean you owe tax — it means you **file IRS Form 709** (the gift tax return) by the following April 15. In most cases the form just tracks the excess against your lifetime exemption rather than producing a bill.

The lifetime gift and estate exemption is **$15 million per individual for 2026** under the law signed this year (the One Big Beautiful Bill Act), so a married couple can shield up to $30 million combined. You only owe actual gift tax once your total lifetime gifts eat through that $15 million. For the vast majority of families, Form 709 is a reporting exercise, not a tax bill.

## The easy stuff people forget is also excluded

Some gifts don't count toward the exclusion at all:

- **Tuition paid directly to a school** — unlimited, no filing, no effect on your $19,000.
- **Medical bills paid directly to the provider** — same deal.
- **Gifts to a spouse** — generally unlimited.

So paying a grandchild's college tuition by sending the check straight to the university doesn't touch your annual exclusion. Writing the same check to the grandchild's bank account does.

## The recipient owes nothing

Gifts aren't income. The person receiving the money owes no federal income tax on it and files nothing. The giver — not the recipient — is the one with any filing responsibility, and only when the exclusion is exceeded.

## Practical checklist for the rest of 2026

1. **List who you plan to help this year** — kids, grandkids, anyone else.
2. **Stay at or under $19,000 per recipient per donor** ($38,000 from a couple) to keep things paperwork-free.
3. **Pay tuition or medical costs directly to the institution** when that's the goal — it bypasses the limit entirely.
4. **Finish transfers by December 31** so they land in the right tax year.
5. **If you exceed the limit**, plan to file Form 709 by April 15, 2027 — and don't panic; for almost everyone it's reporting only.

The annual exclusion is one of the few tax-planning tools that costs nothing to use. The only requirement is timing: once December 31 passes, that year's $19,000-per-person allowance is gone for good.
