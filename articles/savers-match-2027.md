# The IRS Is Mailing Notices About a $1,000 Retirement Match — Here's What It Means

> Starting in 2027, the federal government will match part of your retirement contributions directly into your account — up to $1,000 a year per person. The IRS has begun sending notices (Form CP321J) to taxpayers who might qualify. Here's who it's for, how it works, and what to do right now.

Reviewed: October 9, 2026

## The headline: a match, not a tax credit

Congress created the **Saver's Match** in the SECURE 2.0 Act of 2022. It takes effect for contributions made in the 2027 tax year, and it replaces the old Saver's Credit after the 2026 tax year.

The difference matters. The old Saver's Credit was a *nonrefundable* tax credit: it could lower your tax bill, but if you owed little or no tax — common for lower earners — it did you no good. The Saver's Match is a direct contribution: the federal government puts money *into your retirement account*. You get the benefit even if your tax liability is zero.

How much: the government matches **50% of your first $2,000 in qualifying retirement contributions per year**, which works out to a maximum of **$1,000 per eligible person per year**. If you're married filing jointly and both spouses qualify, each can earn the match on their own contributions — up to $2,000 per household. The matching money grows tax-deferred inside the account, just like your own contributions.

## Who qualifies

The program is aimed squarely at low- and moderate-income savers. For 2027, the full 50% match goes to:

- **Single filers** with modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) of $20,500 or less
- **Heads of household** with MAGI of $30,750 or less
- **Married couples filing jointly** with MAGI of $41,000 or less

The benefit phases out as income rises, ending entirely at $35,500 (single), $53,250 (head of household), and $71,000 (joint). The IRS says these thresholds will be adjusted for inflation after 2027, and exact final figures are subject to pending regulations — so treat the cutoffs as strong guidance, not final law.

Beyond income, you must also:

- Be at least 18 years old by the end of the tax year
- Not be enrolled as a full-time student
- Not be claimed as a dependent on someone else's return
- Be a U.S. resident for tax purposes

Qualifying contributions include money put into a 401(k) or similar workplace plan, or into a traditional or Roth IRA.

## Why the IRS is writing to you now

The IRS is sending CP321J notices to people who claimed the Saver's Credit on their 2025 tax return, or whose 2025 income put them in the range where the new match *might* apply. The letter is informational — two things to know about it:

1. **Getting the letter doesn't guarantee you'll qualify.** Your eligibility is decided by your 2027 income, age, filing status, and contributions — not by whether you got a notice.
2. **You don't need to do anything with the letter in 2026.** There's no form to file, no phone call to make, no fee to pay.

Be skeptical of anyone who contacts you *about* this program and asks for personal details or money. Scammers love to impersonate new government benefits. The IRS initiates contact by mail and will not demand payment or sensitive information to "activate" your match.

## The practical playbook

**If you're in the income range, the strategy is simple: save first, the match follows.** The match only rewards money you actually contribute. Contribute $2,000 to a qualifying account in 2027 and the government adds up to $1,000 on top — an instant 50% return on top of any investment growth. Contribute nothing and you get nothing.

**If you don't have a workplace plan, an IRA counts.** Open a traditional or Roth IRA at any major brokerage — it takes minutes and usually has no minimum. This is the simplest path for gig workers, freelancers, and anyone whose employer doesn't offer a 401(k). The Treasury is also building a dedicated low-cost IRA portal (TrumpIRA.gov) aimed at workers without employer plans, with a target launch around January 2027.

**Start the habit in 2026.** The match doesn't apply to 2026 contributions, but building the savings habit now means you'll actually be contributing when 2027 arrives. Even $100 a month into an IRA — $1,200 for the year — would earn a $600 match at the full 50% rate.

**You'll claim it on your 2027 return.** The IRS is creating a new form, Form 8880-A, for the 2027 tax return (filed in 2028). Keep records of your 2027 contributions, and make sure your tax preparer or tax software knows to look for the match.

**Don't bank on it until the rules are final.** Treasury issued a notice of intent to propose regulations in 2026, which means some details — final income thresholds, exact claiming mechanics — can still move. Use the numbers above as planning guidance and check the IRS website when you're ready to file your 2027 return.

## The bottom line

The Saver's Match is one of the most generous incentives ever offered to lower-income retirement savers: free federal money, deposited into your own account, regardless of your tax bill. The IRS notice in your mailbox is just a heads-up — the real work is yours: be contributing to a qualifying account in 2027, and be in the income range. A modest contribution next year could literally double half of what you put in. Few financial offers get better than that.
