# Trump Accounts vs. 529 Plans: Which Is Better for Your Kid's Future?

> A new kind of child savings account launched in 2026 with a possible $1,000 government head start. Here's how it stacks up against the classic 529 plan — and how to think about using one, the other, or both.

Reviewed: October 8, 2026

## What a Trump Account is

Trump Accounts are a new type of savings account for kids, created by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act signed into law in 2025. They are structured as a traditional IRA — but for a child, not a retiree. Any U.S. child under 18 with a Social Security number is eligible, and unlike a normal IRA, the child doesn't need any earned income.

The details that matter most:

- **Contribution limit:** up to $5,000 per child per year, combined across all contributors — parents, grandparents, friends, and employers (an employer can contribute up to $2,500 of that cap on an employee's behalf).
- **The $1,000 pilot deposit:** U.S. citizen children born between 2025 and 2028 with a valid Social Security number are eligible for a one-time $1,000 federal seed deposit. It doesn't count against the $5,000 annual cap.
- **Tax treatment:** contributions are not tax-deductible, but money grows tax-deferred. When it's eventually withdrawn, it's taxed under traditional IRA rules — and pulling money out before age 59½ generally triggers a 10% penalty, except for qualifying uses like higher education expenses or a first-home purchase.
- **Lock-up:** the account is locked during the child's "growth period," which ends December 31 of the year the child turns 17. After that, it converts into a regular IRA the young adult controls.
- **Investments:** money goes into low-cost diversified index funds, so it rides the long-term growth of the stock market rather than sitting in cash.

One practical note: as of late 2026, the Treasury has auto-enrolled eligible children, but an account isn't active until a parent or guardian claims it and completes verification. The pilot deposit also requires an affirmative election — the government can't make that choice for you. If your child was born between 2025 and 2028, this is worth doing sooner rather than later: it's a real $1,000 you don't have to earn.

## What a 529 plan is

A 529 plan is the older, more established option. It's a state-run savings plan designed specifically for education: contributions grow tax-free, and withdrawals are tax-free when used for qualified education expenses like tuition, books, and room and board. Many states also offer a state tax deduction or credit for contributions, depending on where you live.

The key tradeoff of a 529 is its narrow purpose. It shines if the money goes toward education. If you pull money out for non-education spending, earnings are taxed and penalized — so it's a purpose-built tool, not a general savings account.

## Head-to-head: the real differences

**Purpose.** A 529 is education-first: college, trade school, K–12 tuition in some cases. A Trump Account is a long-horizon investment account with no spending restrictions once the child controls it — education, a first home, a business, anything. That flexibility is its biggest advantage, and its biggest temptation.

**Taxes.** A 529 gives you tax-free growth and tax-free withdrawals for education. A Trump Account gives you tax-deferred growth, but withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income (minus the portion that was already-taxed contributions). For pure education spending, the 529 usually wins on taxes. For everything else, the Trump Account gives you options the 529 doesn't.

**Access.** A 529 can be tapped for college at 18 with no penalty. A Trump Account stays locked until the growth period ends, and spending it before 59½ without a qualifying exception costs a 10% penalty. In other words: a 529 is an 18-year plan; a Trump Account is closer to a lifetime plan.

**The free money.** Nothing in the 529 world matches the Trump Account's $1,000 pilot deposit for eligible children. It's a genuine head start — money that arrives before you've contributed a cent.

## The honest answer: it's not either/or

For most families, these two accounts do different jobs, and using both is perfectly reasonable:

- **If education is the goal,** fund a 529 first. It's the best tax deal for college and trade-school costs, and many states sweeten it further.
- **If your child qualifies for the pilot deposit,** claim the Trump Account regardless. The $1,000 seed costs you nothing and compounds for nearly two decades.
- **If you want flexibility** — savings that could become a down payment, seed capital, or a retirement head start — additional Trump Account contributions fit that role.

Two things to keep straight. First, Trump Account contributions are not tax-deductible, so don't fund one expecting a tax break this year. Second, the $1,000 pilot is only for the 2025–2028 birth window — children born earlier can still open an account, they just don't get the seed money.

The biggest mistake would be letting the shiny new option distract from the boring basics: the account that actually gets funded wins. An automatic monthly contribution to either account beats a perfect strategy you never start.
