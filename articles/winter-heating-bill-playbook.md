# The Winter Heating Bill Playbook: 12 Moves to Cut Your Cold-Weather Energy Costs

> Realtor.com is warning about a painful heating season, and utilities are saying the same thing. You can't control fuel prices, but you can control how much of it your home burns — and how much assistance you claim.

Reviewed: October 19, 2026

## 1. Turn the thermostat down when you're asleep or away

The single highest-leverage move is free. The Department of Energy estimates that setting the thermostat back 7–10 degrees for about eight hours a day can cut your heating costs by up to 10% over the year. Set it before bed and when the house is empty; a programmable or smart thermostat does this automatically so you don't have to remember.

## 2. Get on budget billing before the first big bill

Most utilities let you spread winter costs across the year in equal monthly payments. You still pay for everything you use, but January and February stop being $300 gut-punches. Call your utility and ask — enrollment often closes once heating season starts, so do it this month.

## 3. Stop drafts at their source

A dollar of heat leaking under the front door costs the same as a dollar of heat from the furnace. Run your hand around exterior doors, windows, and baseboards on a windy day. Weatherstripping and door sweeps are cheap, and a tube of caulk handles gaps around window frames and where pipes enter the house.

## 4. Seal the chimney when it's not in use

An open fireplace damper is an open window to the roof. Close it every time the fire is fully out, and consider an inflatable chimney plug if you rarely use the fireplace — just remember it's in there before lighting the next fire.

## 5. Keep the furnace or boiler serviced

A heating system with a dirty filter or neglected burners runs longer to produce the same heat. Change the filter monthly during heating season and get the system serviced once a year before the cold arrives. If it makes new noises, short-cycles, or heats unevenly, that's a repair to make now — mid-January emergency calls are never cheaper.

## 6. Bleed the radiators (if you have them)

Radiators with air trapped inside heat unevenly — hot at the bottom, cold at the top. A radiator key costs almost nothing; open the bleed valve until water runs steadily, then close it. Do this at the start of the season and your system runs shorter cycles.

## 7. Let the sun in, keep curtains closed at night

South-facing windows are free daytime heat. Open the curtains in the morning, then close everything at dusk — windows are the thinnest part of your insulation. Heavy or thermal curtains help more than thin ones.

## 8. Check your attic insulation

Heat rises, and an under-insulated attic is the most common place it escapes. If you can see the ceiling joists poking through the insulation, you probably don't have enough. Adding insulation is one of the few upgrades that pays for itself quickly — but get multiple quotes if you're hiring it out.

## 9. Ask your utility about a free home energy audit

Many utilities offer free or discounted home energy assessments, sometimes with free weatherization supplies thrown in. Search your utility's name plus "energy audit" or call and ask. Some states also run weatherization assistance programs that pay for insulation, sealing, and furnace repairs for qualifying households.

## 10. Look into LIHEAP if money is tight

The federal Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) helps qualifying households pay heating and cooling bills. Funding is distributed through state and local agencies, and applications typically open in the fall — meaning now. Search "LIHEAP" plus your state to find your local office and the income limits. The program can also cover crisis assistance for households at risk of shutoff.

## 11. Lower the water heater temperature

Your water heater is often the second-biggest energy user in the house. Most are set at 140 degrees; 120 is hot enough for showers, laundry, and dishwashers in most homes, and it lowers the standby cost all winter. Insulate the tank and the first few feet of hot-water pipe for extra savings.

## 12. Compare delivery and supply rates if your state allows choice

In deregulated states you can sometimes choose your electricity or natural-gas supplier separately from the utility that delivers it. The utility's own default rate is the safe benchmark — compare third-party offers against it, watch for teaser rates that reset higher, and never let a door-to-door sales rep rush you into a contract.

## The bottom line

You don't need a renovation to take the edge off heating season. Drop the thermostat on a schedule, seal the leaks you can feel with your hand, get on budget billing, and check whether LIHEAP or a free audit is available in your area. Do the free things this week and the rest before the first real cold snap.
