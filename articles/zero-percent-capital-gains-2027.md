# Pay 0% Capital Gains Tax in 2027: How Much You Can Earn and Still Owe Nothing

> The IRS just set the 2027 capital gains brackets: a single filer can have up to $51,050 in taxable income and pay 0% on long-term gains — $102,100 for a married couple. Add the standard deduction, and the real number is even higher. Here's how the 0% bracket works and who can actually use it.

Reviewed: November 11, 2026

## The 2027 numbers

The IRS released its 2027 inflation adjustments on November 5, 2026 (Revenue Procedure 2026-35). The long-term capital gains rates themselves didn't change — still 0%, 15%, and 20% — but the income thresholds moved up about 3.2%.

| Filing status | 0% rate up to | 15% rate up to | 20% rate above |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Single | **$51,050** | $563,200 | $563,200 |
| Married filing jointly | **$102,100** | $633,600 | $633,600 |
| Head of household | **$68,350** | $598,400 | $598,400 |
| Married filing separately | **$51,050** | $563,200 | $563,200 |

These thresholds apply to **taxable income** — income after deductions, not your total earnings. And they apply only to long-term gains: investments held **more than one year**. Sell something you held for less than a year and the gain is taxed as ordinary income, at your regular rate. Qualified dividends use these same brackets.

## The number that matters more: after the standard deduction

Because the 0% threshold is measured on taxable income, the standard deduction stretches it further. The 2027 standard deduction is $16,600 for single filers and $33,200 for married couples filing jointly.

That means a single filer with no other income could realize up to **$67,650 in long-term gains** in 2027 ($51,050 + $16,600) and owe $0 in federal capital gains tax. A married couple filing jointly could realize up to **$135,300**. The 0% rate covers the first slice; only gains above the threshold get taxed at 15%.

One honest caveat: "up to $67,650 in gains" assumes gains are your only income. If you also earn a salary, that ordinary income fills up the 0% bracket first, and your gains stack on top of it. Every dollar of wages shrinks the room left for tax-free gains.

## Who can actually use the 0% bracket

The 0% bracket isn't a loophole — it's the tax code working as designed, and it helps most in low-income years:

- **Early retirees in the gap years.** The stretch between stopping work and starting Social Security or required minimum distributions is the classic window: little ordinary income, lots of room in the 0% bracket, and a chance to realize gains or rebalance a portfolio tax-free.
- **A low-income or no-income year.** A sabbatical, a year between jobs, or a year you took time off to care for family can be the cheapest year to sell appreciated investments.
- **Students and young investors.** If your taxable income is below the threshold, selling a long-held investment can cost nothing in federal tax — useful when you need the cash for a down payment or a career move.

## Tax-gain harvesting, in plain English

Tax-loss harvesting gets all the attention, but the mirror image is worth knowing: in a year your income is low, you can deliberately sell appreciated investments, pay 0% on the gain, and immediately buy them back. The sale resets your cost basis higher, so a future sale produces a smaller taxable gain. The wash-sale rule that blocks quick re-buys after a loss does **not** apply to gains — you can buy back the same day.

This only makes sense when you actually have room in the 0% bracket and a reason to reset the basis. Don't sell just because the rate is zero; sell because the sale fits a plan you already had.

## Three things that can trip you up

- **State taxes are separate.** The 0% rate is federal only. Most states tax capital gains as ordinary income, so check your state's rules before assuming the sale is free.
- **Gains can push other income into a higher bracket.** Your gains sit on top of your ordinary income, so a big sale can push your last dollars of wages or retirement withdrawals into a higher rate — and can affect things like Medicare premiums or the taxation of Social Security benefits.
- **The 3.8% net investment income tax.** Above $200,000 of income for single filers ($250,000 joint), an extra 3.8% applies to investment income. It doesn't touch the 0% bracket, but it matters if your total income is high.

## Practical checklist

1. **Check your holding period** — more than one year before you sell, or the gain is ordinary income.
2. **Estimate your 2027 taxable income first** — wages fill the 0% bracket before gains do.
3. **Add the standard deduction** — $16,600 single, $33,200 joint in 2027 — to find your real tax-free room.
4. **Sell in a low-income year when you can** — gap years between work and retirement are the best window.
5. **Remember state tax** — the federal 0% rate doesn't mean a $0 total bill.

The 0% capital gains bracket is one of the few places in the tax code where timing alone can erase a tax bill. The only requirement is knowing your income for the year before you sell.
